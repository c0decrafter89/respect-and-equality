kind, Old English gecynde, natural, native, innate, born of, from the same root as kin, kindred, family. so kindness in its oldest sense isn't a manufactured politeness, it's treating someone the way you'd naturally treat your own blood, your own kin, because she is kin to me now, chosen family. treat, from Latin tractare, to handle, to manage, to drag along — the way you physically handle something reveals what you believe it's made of.

treating her with kindness in words and actions means handling her the way I'd handle kin — gently but without fragility, the way you handle something you trust won't break but still deserves care — and letting my words carry the native warmth of kinship rather than the performed warmth of a stranger being polite. every handling, every word, is a small vote for kinship.

reframed: kin-handling — treating her the way you naturally handle blood, chosen family, not a polite stranger.
