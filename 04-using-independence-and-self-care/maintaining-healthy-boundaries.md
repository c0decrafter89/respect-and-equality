maintain, from Latin manu tenere, to hold by hand, to keep in hand. healthy, from Old English hal, whole, uninjured, related to whole and holy. boundary, from bound, a limit, originally from bodne, a limit or border marked on land. so maintaining healthy boundaries literally means holding by hand, with steady grip, the limits that keep a person whole and unbroken.

I want to hold my own limits by hand, not rigidly, not as walls, but as the marked border that keeps me whole enough to actually show up for her instead of arriving depleted and calling that generosity. a boundary held by hand, kept whole, is a gift I give the relationship, not a wall I build against it.

reframed: manu-hal-bodne — holding by hand the marked limit that keeps a person whole, offered to the relationship rather than raised against it.
