apology, Greek apologia, apo- away, from, and logos, speech, reasoned account — originally not an admission of fault at all but a defense, a reasoned speech given in one's own account. somewhere the word flipped, and now to apologize means almost the opposite: to give an account not in my own defense but in defense of the harm done to someone else, to reason it clearly from their side. amend, Latin emendare, e- out, and menda, a fault, a blemish — to take the fault out.

so when I apologize I try to remember the word wants me to give a reasoned account, not a hollow "sorry" tossed out to end the discomfort, but an actual accounting of what happened and why it landed the way it did. and amends means doing the work to take the fault out, not just naming it, actually removing the blemish through changed action.

reframed: apo-logos-e-menda — a reasoned account given from her side of it, followed by the actual work of taking the fault out.
