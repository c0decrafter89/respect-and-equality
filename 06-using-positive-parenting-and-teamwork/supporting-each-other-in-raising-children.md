support, sub-portare, to carry from below. raise, from Old Norse reisa, to cause to rise, to lift up. child, Old English cild, related to womb, the one born of. so raising children in its bones means causing them to rise, lifting them up from underneath, and supporting each other in that means we take turns being the underneath, the sub-portare, so neither of us is always the one lifting alone.

I think about this and want it to mean we trade off who's underneath on a given day, who's tired and needs to be lifted themselves before they can lift the kid, and neither of us keeping score of the carrying, because the load being lifted is a person who didn't ask to need lifting, it's just what rising requires.

reframed: sub-reisa-trading — taking turns being the underneath that causes a small rising, without keeping score of who carried more.
