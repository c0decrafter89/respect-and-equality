fair, Old English fæger, beautiful, pleasing, clear — long before fair meant equitable it meant lovely to look at, unclouded. considerate, Latin considerare, con- with, and sidus, sideris, star — to observe the stars together, to study something as carefully as ancient navigators studied the sky. so being fair and considerate means keeping things clear, unclouded between us, while studying her situation as carefully as a star chart before I judge it.

I want fairness to mean the clear, unclouded fæger kind, not a cold calculation but something that actually looks lovely and right when you step back from it, and considerateness to mean I actually study her sky, her particular constellation of pressures, before I decide what's fair inside it.
